The idea

One story, not fifty illustrations

Every joke in the song is about Tiff being lost, distracted or overwhelmed, and every chorus is about her being brilliant. The film ties those together with a GPS. In Verse 1 Adrian is lost because of her. In the Bridge the map zooms out, and the GPS pin marked HOME drops onto her, not the house.

Two drawing modes

Gag mode: chibi proportions, comic marks and big reactions, for every joke. Heart mode: gentler proportions and a warm peach wash, for the sincere beats. The film slides from gag to heart and back, so the switch itself tells the audience when to laugh and when to feel.

One flop, one flex

Each time Tiff fails at something, she’s shown being excellent at something nearby. That keeps her lovable and competent, which the brief asks for. The ledger below tracks every pair.

Callbacks, not nouns

Thirteen recurring motifs (exit 41, spiral eyes, the begging Post button, the rain cloud) are set up early and paid off later. That’s what makes it play as one story.

    Production

    Higgsfield pipeline

    The model names below are what Higgsfield recommends for this kind of work today. The ‘Gen’ field on each shot says which route that shot takes.

    Style is locked

    Simple doodle comic in mauve ink: black line, white fills, dusty mauve-pink shading, plus small accent colours (lavender for Tiff, mustard for Adrian, cream and red for Spinda). The fixed V1-01 car swatch is the reference shot; pass it as an image reference with every keyframe so the look stays put.

    Build character sheets

    The look is locked: simple doodle comic in mauve ink with small accent colours. Use Higgsfield’s character-sheet workflow for Tiff, Adrian, each cat and the Spinda plush: turnaround, expressions, chibi variant, spiral eyes. Feed these sheets as image references into every keyframe after this.

    Keyframe each shot

    Paste the shot’s Copy prompt into Nano Banana Pro or FLUX 3 with the character sheets attached. Approve the still before you spend anything on video.

    Animate

    I2V shots: Seedance 2.5 (reference mode) from the approved keyframe. S+E shots (Spinda multiplying, melting, versions merging, the feast): MiniMax H3 or Seedance 2.0 with a start frame and an end frame. Turn the model’s own audio off, since the song is the soundtrack. Most of these models generate at least 4–5 seconds, while many shots here are 2–3 seconds, so generate a little long and trim to the beat. Draft at 480p, then finalise the takes you keep.

    Text goes in the edit

    Speech bubbles, notifications, the quiz card, EXIT 41, the role cards and the end card should be added in your editor. Video models garble lettering. The prompts already say ‘no text’ and leave space for it.

    Cut to the song

    Lay the song on the timeline first and drop in the cuts at the start times on this board. Big hits worth landing exactly: the chorus drops (0:52.3, 1:55.4, 3:09.7), the stop beats before “oddball life lovely” (1:06, 2:09), the 2-second silence at 3:52 and the final drop at 3:54.8.

    Reuse on purpose

    Shots marked Edit are re-cuts or image edits of earlier keyframes (Chorus 2, outro callbacks). Locking the C1-06 living-room framing lets you add clutter for each chorus without regenerating the scene.
